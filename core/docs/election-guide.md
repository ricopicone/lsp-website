# Running a Board election

The Election Administrator runs the School's election of its Board of
Directors. The Board appoints the administrator on Board → Appointments; the
administrator should be neither a sitting director nor a candidate, and may
not nominate themselves.

## What the bylaws say

Article 3, Section 1: the Board is "elected from among its Candidates and
Analysts." Members are told of the election and invited to apply, with a
motivation letter "clearly stating their motivation, their interests and what
they can bring to the Board and the School." The Board reviews the
applications, publishes the list of candidates with their letters, and
communicates the timeline and means of vote. "If there are seven or fewer
candidates running who satisfy the other requirements of the Board, no
election will take place." The Board has five to seven directors, at least
three of them analysts, and results go to the membership within one week of
the end of voting.

Article 12: analysts, scholars, candidates and pre-candidates "who have paid
their fees are voting members."

## How an election runs

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| Draft | You set the dates and rules. Members see nothing. |
| Nominations open | Every voter is emailed the call. Analysts, scholars, candidates and candidate scholars nominate themselves with a letter, and can edit or withdraw until nominations close. |
| Board review | Board members accept or decline each nomination on their own review page. |
| Slate published | You publish the slate once every nomination is decided. Accepted candidates are told, and members can read the letters. |
| Voting open | The voter roll is frozen and every voter is emailed. Voters rank the candidates and cast one final ballot. |
| Voting closed | You run the count. |
| Counted | You check the count, then publish the results. |
| Results published | Voters are emailed the results. |

If the accepted candidates don't outnumber the seats, the Overview says so and
offers **Close as elected without a vote**, once the Board has decided every
nomination and until voting opens. Whether to use it is your call. If the
voting date has already arrived, the site won't publish an uncontested slate
on its own (that would open a vote at once): close without a vote, or move
the voting dates later to hold one.

A count with no ballots elects nobody; seats are never filled by lot from
nothing.

## Dates

You set four dates: nominations open, nominations close, voting opens, voting
closes. They open and close each window by themselves; the emails go out
within about five minutes of the time you set. **Open now** and **Close now**
on the Overview do the same thing immediately. If the site was down for the
whole nomination window, the call is never sent late, since it would invite
nominations that have closed; the record says so, and you can move the
dates and write to voters yourself.

A date that has already passed can't be changed, so a closed window stays
closed. To extend an open window, move its closing date later. Voting never
opens before the slate is published: if the voting date arrives first, voting
opens the moment you publish. Until the slate is published the voting dates
stay movable even once they have passed, since that window has never opened,
so a Board review that runs late only needs new voting dates. Every change is
written to the record on the Overview.

## Nominations by email

If the Board sends its own call for nominations, untick **Email the call
for nominations** in Settings before nominations open, so members don't get
two different calls. Members can still nominate themselves on the site.

When a nomination arrives by email, enter it on the Nominations tab with the
member's email address and their letter. You can do this until the slate is
published, so a letter sent before the deadline can be entered after it. The
member gets the usual confirmation, the nomination shows who entered it, and
the Board reviews it like any other. Only analysts, scholars, candidates and
candidate scholars can be entered, and you can't change a nomination the
Board has already decided.

## Board review

The Board accepts or declines each nomination, with a note only the Board and
you can see. **A declined applicant is contacted personally by a Board
member**, as the bylaws require; the site sends them nothing.

## The voter roll

The roll starts as everyone with a voting role (analyst, scholar, candidate,
candidate scholar, pre-candidate, pre-candidate scholar, member) in active
standing. "Paid their fees" is your judgment, not the site's: the roll shows
each person's dues for this year as information, and you add or remove
people with a reason that is kept in the record. The roll is frozen when
voting opens. After that you can still add someone who was missed, and you
can remove someone only if they haven't voted.

While voting is open the roll shows who has voted, never when, and
**Remind those who haven't voted** emails only them.

## Counting

**Single transferable vote (STV)** is the default. A candidate who reaches
the quota is elected and the votes they don't need pass to each voter's next
choice; when nobody reaches it, the candidate with the fewest votes is
excluded and their votes pass on the same way. The site follows the Scottish
STV rules of 2007, and the Count tab shows every stage.

**Most votes** gives one vote to each of a ballot's top choices, as many as
there are seats, and elects the highest totals.

A tie the rules can't break is decided by lot. The site draws it and records
the draw so it can be checked; you can also redo it publicly and record that.

If the elected analysts plus the continuing directors who are analysts make
fewer than three, the Count tab warns you. The site doesn't change the
result: the remedy, such as a Director-at-Large appointment, is the Board's.

## What members see

Settings decide how much. While voting is open members see the status, and
the turnout if you choose. After the count they see the winners, and the
stage-by-stage count if you choose. Nobody sees a running tally while voting
is open.

## What ballot secrecy covers

No page on the site shows how anyone voted, including these pages. You see
whether each person has voted, never when.

A ballot is stored without the voter's name and without a time, under a
random identifier, so it can't be traced to a voter even by someone reading
the database. A separate record says only that a person has voted, which is
what the roll shows and what stops a second ballot. The cost, which the
Board chose, is that a ballot is final: nobody can see or change it once it
is cast, the voter included. The ballot-recorded email doesn't list the
voter's choices either.
