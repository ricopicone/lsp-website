# Groups at the LSP — a guide

Every collaborative body the school runs online is a **Group**. This guide
explains who starts each kind, who approves it, how people join, who runs it,
and how it ends. It's for staff, the Board, the Programming Committee, and
faculty — no technical background needed.

## The five kinds

| Kind | What it is | Who starts it |
|---|---|---|
| **Cartel** | A few members + a "plus-one", formed around a shared question | Any member proposes it |
| **Working Group** | A standing, task-oriented group serving the school | The Board charters it |
| **Committee** | A standing body that carries the school's work | Set up by staff (foundational) |
| **Seminar** | A year-long teaching seminar led by faculty | Faculty propose, or the PC builds it |
| **Reading Group** | A group reading a shared text together | A standing, openly-joinable group |

The Board, the Programming Committee, and the **Meeting of Analysts** are all
*committees* — specific instances of the Committee kind, not kinds of their own.

## What every Group has

A **Workspace** — one page per Group, with tabs for an overview, a private
discussion, shared works and files, a schedule, tasks, and settings. What
differs between kinds is how a Group is created and who runs it, not its
Workspace.

## Two ideas that apply to every Group

**Managers.** A Group's *managers* are the people who can edit its roster,
settings, and schedule (and, for committees, its charter). You manage a Group
if you are: one of its leaders (chair, co-chair, plus-one, faculty, or
organizer); on the Programming Committee or LSP Staff; on the Board; or a site
administrator. One safeguard: **a Group must always keep at least one leader** —
the last leader can't leave or be removed until another is in place.

**Active vs. archive-only.** Active members take part fully. *Archive-only*
access is read-only — you can read past discussion and released works but not
post. It's what you keep after you leave, after a Seminar year ends without
re-enrolling, or after a Group is archived.

**Archiving.** Archiving dissolves a Group: it goes read-only (no new posts or
members) but nothing is deleted, and past members keep read access. Managers can
reactivate it later. This is the graceful way to end a Group.

## The kinds in detail

### Cartels

- **Create:** any member clicks **Propose a cartel** and describes the guiding
  question (and may name people to invite).
- **Approve:** the **Programming Committee** approves or declines. The **Cartel
  Coordinator** can add feedback, but the PC decides. A declined proposal can be
  edited and resubmitted.
- **Join:** accept an invitation; **apply** to an open cartel (a member then
  accepts you); or be named its **plus-one** (who may be someone outside the
  school, invited to create an account).
- **Run:** the members. Any member can close it to new applicants, reopen it, or
  archive it.
- **Schedule:** none — cartels are self-directed (by design).

### Working Groups

- **Create:** a **Board** member clicks **New working group**, names it, sets
  its **chair**, and adds starting members. No separate approval — the Board's
  creation is the authorization.
- **Join:** managers add people from the Workspace **Settings** tab. No open
  join.
- **Run:** the chair, plus the Board and LSP Staff.
- **Schedule:** members and managers add meetings on the Schedule tab.

### Committees

Committees are foundational — set up by staff, not proposed. Members are
**appointed**: a manager adds them on the Settings tab and sets each role
(chair, secretary, treasurer, …). Managers — the chair, the Board, the
Programming Committee, and LSP Staff — can edit the committee's **charter**
right on the Settings tab. Committee members and managers schedule meetings on
the Schedule tab.

**Named committees.** The Board, the Programming Committee, and the Meeting of
Analysts already exist as committees.

**Automatic membership (the Meeting of Analysts).** A committee can be set so
that everyone in a given role is automatically a member. The Meeting of Analysts
works this way: **every Analyst belongs to it automatically** — no one joins.
Its schedule is set by its chair (or the Board / LSP Staff); ordinary Analyst
members see the schedule but don't edit it. (It is *not* the spring Day of
Assembly, which is a separate event.)

### Seminars

- **Create — two ways:** the **Programming Committee** builds a seminar directly
  in the Program admin; **or** faculty click **Propose a seminar** (on the
  Program page) with the title, dates, format, and instructors — optionally as a
  **new year of an existing seminar**.
- **Approve a proposal:** the **Programming Committee**, on the **Proposals**
  tab of the Program admin. Approving **creates the seminar** and adds it to the
  year's program (you publish the program separately). Declining records a
  reason the faculty member can see and respond to.
- **Join:** students **register** (and pay, unless covered); faculty are listed
  as instructors.
- **Each year:** a seminar keeps the same Workspace across years. Students must
  **re-enroll** each year to stay active; otherwise they keep read-only access
  to past materials.
- **Run:** the faculty (edit the description, set pricing codes, see the roster)
  and the Programming Committee.

### Reading Groups

- **Create:** a standing, openly-joinable group.
- **Join:** any member **joins with one click** (no approval, no payment). If a
  reading group runs a **paid year**, members **register** for that year
  instead.
- **Run:** **organizers** (plus LSP Staff and the Programming Committee), who can
  open a new paid year.
- **Schedule:** organizers and members add meetings on the Schedule tab.

## How do I…?

- **Propose a cartel** (any member) — **Groups → Cartels → Propose a cartel**.
- **Review cartel proposals** (PC / Coordinator) — **Groups → Cartels → Review
  proposals**.
- **Start a working group** (Board) — **Groups → Working Groups → New working
  group**.
- **Propose a seminar** (faculty) — **Program page → Propose a seminar**; track
  or resubmit it on the same page.
- **Approve a seminar proposal** (PC) — **Program admin → Proposals →
  Approve & mint** (or Decline with a reason), then publish the program.
- **Edit a committee's charter** (chair / Board / staff) — the committee's
  Workspace → **Settings** tab.
- **Add or remove a member** (managers) — the Group's **Settings** tab →
  **Members** (you can't remove the last leader without naming another first).
- **Leave a Group** (any member) — **Leave group** at the top of the Workspace.
- **Archive or reactivate a Group** (managers) — the **Settings** tab.
