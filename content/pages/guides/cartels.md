A cartel is a small group that forms around a shared **guiding question**—a few
members who work a question together for a while, in the Lacanian tradition of
the cartel and its **plus-one**. Cartels form on the members' own cadence, any
time; they don't wait on the seminar calendar.

## Forming a cartel

Any member can propose one. Go to **[Propose a cartel](/cartels/propose/)** (or the "Propose a
cartel" button on the Cartels page) and give it a name, the guiding question it
forms around, and—optionally—a few members you'd like to invite.

A proposal goes to the **Program Committee**, which reviews it; the Cartel
Coordinator may add advisory feedback. Once approved, the cartel **opens**. A
declined proposal can be edited and resubmitted.

## The plus-one

Every cartel has a **plus-one**—the function, distinct from the working members,
that attends to the cartel's relation to its own question and to the School. A
cartel has one internal plus-one (a member in that role) and may also recognize
an external plus-one as a guest.

## Joining

Once a cartel is open, there are two ways in:

- **By invitation**—the members who were invited at proposal time accept and
  join directly.
- **By application**—any member can ask to join an open cartel (with a short
  message); the cartel's current members review and accept or decline.

Members can also **close** a cartel to new applications when it's full.

## The workspace

An open cartel has its own shared space, reached from the Cartels page. It
gathers the group's work in one place: a **discussion** channel and live
**chat**, shared **documents**, **files**, a **schedule** for meetings, a
**decisions** register, and **tasks**—plus the roster.

Cartels are **leaderless**: there's no lead role, so the group runs itself.
Any member can edit the cartel's details, record a decision, and manage the
shared work.

## Where cartels appear

- The **[Program](/program/)** lists the cartels active in each academic year,
  with their guiding questions.
- The **[Cartels](/groups/cartels/)** page shows the cartels you belong to and
  lets you browse and propose.

A cartel's name and guiding question are visible to members; its roster, posts,
and files stay private to the cartel.
