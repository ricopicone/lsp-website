*Palimpsest*, from the Greek *palimpsestos* — "rubbed again" — names a parchment
or tablet "written upon or inscribed two or three times, the previous text or
texts having been imperfectly erased and still remaining partly visible."

The School takes the palimpsest as a founding genre: both of its founding papers
bear the name — André Patsalides's *Palimpseste* and Adler's *Palimpsest: Viewing
the Unconscious from Another Time and Place*, each written in 1992. Patsalides calls
the palimpsest a *work-text*: a draft imposed on prior texts that must be reworked
and interpreted — clarifying one's imaginary position toward the text, assuming
responsibility for transforming what is written, and enacting the subjective
position that transformation produces. What is at stake, he writes, "is not merely
a change in the edition of a text, but rather an effective positioning of oneself
in regards to the foundation of the School."

To write a palimpsest, then, is to read Freud and Lacan as themselves
palimpsests — texts a century old, overlaid by later misreadings and
translations, their earlier layers still showing through — and, in reworking
them, to take up a position of one's own within the School's ongoing work. In a
member's formation, the palimpsest is the demande that opens the passage from
Precandidate to Candidate: the member's Advisor presents it, with a
recommendation, to the Meeting of the Analysts.
