# A guide to Groups at the LSP

This guide explains how **Groups** work on the school's website — who can start
them, who approves them, how people join, who runs them, and how they wind down.
It's written for staff, the Board, the Programming Committee, and faculty. No
technical background needed.

## What "Groups" are

A **Group** is any of the collaborative bodies the school runs online. They all
share the same home page on the site — a **Workspace** — with tabs for an
overview, discussion, shared works and files, a schedule, tasks, and settings.
What differs between kinds of Group is *how they come into being and who runs
them*.

The kinds:

- **Cartels** — small groups (a few members plus a "plus-one") formed around a
  shared question.
- **Working Groups** — standing, task-oriented groups serving the school's
  organization (chartered by the Board).
- **Committees** — the standing bodies that carry the school's work (the Board,
  the Programming Committee, and others).
- **Seminars** — year-long teaching seminars led by faculty.
- **Reading Groups** — groups reading a shared text together.
- **The Meeting of Analysts** — a standing body that every Analyst belongs to
  automatically.

Every Group has a **Workspace** with a private discussion channel, a place for
shared works and files, and (where relevant) a schedule. Who can *see* a Group
and who can *post* in it depends on the kind and on membership.

## The shape every Group shares

Behind the differences, every Group moves through the same arc:

1. **Created** — someone starts it.
2. **Approved** — for some kinds, a reviewing body says yes.
3. **Joined** — people become members (by invitation, application, registration,
   appointment, or automatically).
4. **Run** — the people in charge manage the roster, the discussion, the
   schedule, and the group's text.
5. **Wound down** — members can leave; the whole Group can be **archived**
   (dissolved) when it's done.

### Who can run a Group ("managers")

Throughout this guide, a Group's **managers** are the people who can edit its
roster, settings, schedule, and (for committees) charter. A person is a manager
of a Group if any of these is true:

- they hold a **leadership role** in that Group (chair, co-chair, plus-one,
  faculty, or organizer); **or**
- they're on the **Programming Committee** or are **LSP Staff**; **or**
- they're on the **Board** (the Board can oversee any standing Group); **or**
- they're a site administrator.

One safeguard runs everywhere: **a Group must always keep at least one leader.**
The site won't let the last chair/leader leave, be removed, or be demoted until
someone else is made a leader first.

### Active vs. archived members

- **Active members** can post in the discussion and take part fully.
- **Archive-only access** is read-only: you can still read a Group's past
  discussion and released works, but you can't post. This is what someone keeps
  after they leave, after a Seminar term ends without re-enrolling, or after a
  Group is archived.

### Archiving (dissolving) a Group

Archiving a Group makes it **read-only**: no new posts, no new members, but
nothing is deleted — past members keep read access to the history. A manager can
**reactivate** an archived Group later. Archiving is the graceful way to end a
Group that has run its course.

## Each kind, in detail

### Cartels

- **Who creates one:** any member of the school can propose a cartel (there's a
  "Propose a cartel" button in the Cartels section). They describe the guiding
  question and may name specific people to invite.
- **Who approves it:** the **Programming Committee** approves or declines it. The
  **Cartel Coordinator** can add feedback to help — but the Programming
  Committee makes the call. A declined proposal can be edited and resubmitted.
- **How people join:** three ways — accept a special invitation from the
  proposer; **apply** to an open cartel (an existing member then accepts you);
  or be named the cartel's **plus-one** (which can even be someone outside the
  school, who's invited to create an account).
- **Who runs it:** the members themselves. Any member can close the cartel to
  new applicants, reopen it, or archive it.
- **Scheduling:** cartels are self-directed — there's no built-in meeting
  calendar (by design).

### Working Groups

- **Who creates one:** the **Board** (or LSP Staff). There's a "New working
  group" button in the Working Groups section, visible to Board members. The
  creator names the group, sets its **chair**, and adds initial members. No
  separate approval step — the Board's creation is the authorization.
- **How people join:** managers add them from the Group's **Settings tab**
  (the Members section). There's no open join.
- **Who runs it:** the **chair** (and the Board / LSP Staff). They manage the
  roster, schedule meetings, and can archive the group.
- **Scheduling:** members and managers can add meetings on the Schedule tab.

### Committees

- **Who creates one:** committees are foundational — they're set up by staff,
  not proposed. (The Board, the Programming Committee, and the Meeting of
  Analysts already exist.)
- **How people join:** members are **appointed** — a manager adds them from the
  Settings tab and sets each person's role (chair, secretary, treasurer, etc.).
- **Who runs it:** the committee's chair, plus the Board, the Programming
  Committee, and LSP Staff. Managers can **edit the committee's charter** (its
  summary and mandate) right on the Settings tab.
- **Scheduling:** committee members and managers schedule meetings on the
  Schedule tab.

### Seminars

- **Who creates one — two ways:**
  1. The **Programming Committee** can build a seminar directly in the Program
     admin.
  2. **Faculty** can **propose** a seminar (a "Propose a seminar" button appears
     on the Program page for faculty). They give the title, dates, format, and
     instructors, and can mark it as a **new year of an existing seminar**.
- **Who approves a proposal:** the **Programming Committee**, on the
  **Proposals** tab of the Program admin. Approving it **creates the seminar**
  and adds it to that year's program. (You still publish the program separately
  when you're ready for it to go public.) Declining records a reason the faculty
  member can see and respond to.
- **How people join:** students **register** (and pay, unless covered). Faculty
  are listed as the seminar's instructors.
- **Renewing each year:** a seminar keeps the same Workspace across years.
  Students must **re-enroll** each year to stay active; if they don't, they keep
  read-only access to past materials but aren't active in the new term.
- **Who runs it:** the faculty (who can edit the description, set pricing codes,
  and see the roster) and the Programming Committee.
- **Scheduling:** the seminar's class sessions are set on the event itself; the
  Workspace also has its own schedule for internal meetings.

### Reading Groups

- **Who creates one:** reading groups are standing, openly-joinable groups.
- **How people join:** any member can **join with one click** (no approval, no
  payment) for a free reading group. If a reading group runs a **paid year**
  ("term"), members **register** for that term instead.
- **Who runs it:** **organizers** (and LSP Staff / the Programming Committee).
  Organizers can open a new paid term for the year.
- **Scheduling:** organizers and members can add meetings on the Schedule tab.

### The Meeting of Analysts

- **What it is:** a standing body that **every Analyst belongs to
  automatically** — no one has to join.
- **Who runs it:** a **chair** (appointed by a manager through the Members
  section) plus the Board and LSP Staff.
- **Its schedule:** only its chair (or the Board / LSP Staff) can add or change
  meetings; ordinary Analyst members see the schedule but don't edit it.
- **Note:** the Meeting of Analysts is *not* the spring Day of Assembly — the
  Day of Assembly remains a separate event.

## How do I…?

**Propose a cartel** (any member) — Go to **Groups → Cartels**, click **Propose
a cartel**, fill in the name, guiding question, and any people to invite. The
Cartel Coordinator and Programming Committee are notified. Once approved, others
can apply to join.

**Review cartel proposals** (Programming Committee / Cartel Coordinator) — Go to
**Groups → Cartels → Review proposals**. The Programming Committee approves or
declines; the Cartel Coordinator can leave feedback.

**Start a working group** (Board) — Go to **Groups → Working Groups → New
working group**. Name it, set the chair, and add any starting members.

**Propose a seminar** (faculty) — On the **Program** page, click **Propose a
seminar**. Enter the title, dates, format, and instructors. To run another year
of an existing seminar, choose it under "Continue an existing seminar." Track
your proposal's status (and edit/resubmit a declined one) on the same page.

**Approve a seminar proposal** (Programming Committee) — In the **Program
admin**, open the **Proposals** tab. Use **Approve & mint** to create the
seminar, or **Decline** with an optional reason. Then publish the program when
you're ready.

**Edit a committee's charter** (committee chair / Board / staff) — Open the
committee's Workspace, go to the **Settings** tab, and edit the Summary and
Charter.

**Add or remove someone from a Group** (managers) — Open the Group's Workspace →
**Settings** tab → **Members** section. Add by name or email, change roles, or
remove. (You can't remove the last leader without naming another first.)

**Leave a Group** (any member) — Open the Group's Workspace and use **Leave
group** at the top. (The sole remaining leader can't leave until another leader
is in place.)

**Archive (dissolve) a Group** (managers) — On the Group's **Settings** tab, use
**Archive group**. It becomes read-only; past members keep read access. Use
**Reactivate group** on the same tab to bring it back.
